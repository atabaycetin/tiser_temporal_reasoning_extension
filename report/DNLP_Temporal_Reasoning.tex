\begin{abstract}
We reproduce TISER, which elicits reasoning, a timeline, reflection, and an answer, and study three extensions. The first is an inference-time context--memory conflict probe; the second is supervised adaptation to synthetic tennis temporal QA; the third is an evaluation extension measuring original-domain retention and generalization to a separately reserved tennis holdout. Aggregate in-domain reproduction is similar to the paper, despite a large TGQA EM discrepancy. TISER weights and prompting increase adherence to edited context, but reflections rarely name the conflict and order reversal remains difficult. Continued tennis adaptation improves holdout EM from 0.575 to 0.735 over the original adapter. Original-domain retention is inconclusive, so the conditional replay branch is not activated. \href{https://github.com/atabaycetin/tiser_temporal_reasoning_extension/tree/main}{GitHub repository}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Temporal reasoning requires ordering, comparing, and measuring events. TISER~\cite{bazaga2025tiser} trains models to emit tagged reasoning, timeline, reflection, and answer stages. We reproduce its in-domain evaluation and study three complementary extensions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Context--memory conflict}: an inference-only robustness probe that changes the evidence while keeping model weights fixed.
\item \textbf{Tennis adaptation}: a supervised transfer experiment that updates LoRA adapters on synthetic tennis temporal QA.
\item \textbf{Retention and held-out generalization}: an evaluation extension that tests whether the selected tennis adapter preserves original-domain performance and generalizes to a separately reserved holdout; replay is conditional on the retention result.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Problem Definition}

\subsection{TISER Formulation}

For question $q$, context $c$, and answer $a$, TISER generates $y=(r,t,s,a)$: reasoning, timeline, reflection, and final answer. Only the extracted answer is scored. We evaluate TGQA~\cite{xiong2024tgqa}, TempReason L2/L3~\cite{tan2023tempreason}, and TimeQA easy/hard~\cite{chen2021timeqa}; ToT-semantic is treated as OOD.

\subsection{Relation to Prior Work}

Prior work shows that language models vary in whether they follow supplied evidence or parametric knowledge~\cite{xie2024conflict}. Our probe tests controlled temporal edits and explicit conflict acknowledgement. Because continued adaptation risks catastrophic forgetting---loss of prior competence after new learning~\cite{kirkpatrick2017forgetting}---we evaluate tennis gains jointly with original-domain retention.

\subsection{Research Questions}

We ask whether the reproduction recovers the reported in-domain regime. For conflict, we test whether prompt and model conditions change contextual adherence (RQ1), reflection identifies disagreement (RQ2), and behaviour varies by conflict type (RQ3). For tennis adaptation, we test whether continued adaptation improves over the original adapter. For retention and held-out generalization, we test whether that improvement persists on a separately reserved holdout and whether the adapted model preserves original-domain performance sufficiently to avoid replay.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Baseline Reproduction}

We train Qwen2.5-Instruct~\cite{qwen2024qwen25} in the released TISER format and score the extracted answer with exact match (EM) and token F1.

\subsection{Extension 1: Context--Memory Conflict}

\paragraph{Memory elicitation and eligibility.}
We remove context from real-entity items and retain those for which the TISER-trained model recalls the gold answer in at least three of five samples. The benchmark is therefore TISER-selected. The vanilla model independently returns the gold answer verbatim in at least three of five samples for only 203/537 conflict sources (37.8\%); its remaining rows measure use of edited context, but not conflict with that model's demonstrated memory.

\paragraph{Conflict construction.}
Deterministic edits shift dates (C1), substitute an entity from the same broad question-type pool (C2), or reverse intervals (C3). Every genuine conflict changes the context-supported answer relative to TISER recall; separate controls only reorder context and preserve the answer. C2 does not guarantee fine-grained semantic compatibility and can therefore be conspicuous.

\paragraph{Run matrix and measures.}
Without retraining, a $2\times2$ matrix crosses vanilla/TISER weights with standard/TISER prompts. Faithful EM scores the edited answer; original EM scores the pre-edit gold answer, equal to the primary TISER-model memory by construction. Only TISER-weight conditions are guaranteed to contrast the evaluated model's own stable recall with edited context. An audited faithful response without explicit conflict acknowledgement is operationally labelled a \emph{silent override}.

\subsection{Extension 2: Tennis-Domain Adaptation}

\paragraph{Dataset creation and processing.}
GPT-5.5 generated synthetic tennis question--context--answer records and, separately, TISER training traces using the prompts in Appendix~\ref{app:tennis-prompts}. Duplicate groups remain within splits; only training records receive traces. Model audits check semantic answerability and trace support. Data are CC BY 4.0 and code is MIT licensed.

\paragraph{Adaptation strategies.}
At 0.5B we compare prompting and tennis-only training; at 7B we compare the original TISER adapter with continued tennis adaptation.

\subsection{Extension 3: Retention and Held-Out Generalization}

After model selection, we freeze the original (C0) and adapted (C1) adapters. We compare them on 100 seed-42 items per original split and evaluate both once on the separately reserved 113-item tennis holdout. A 10,000-resample paired macro-EM interval triggers replay only if its upper bound is below $-0.02$, stops as non-inferior if its lower bound exceeds $-0.02$, and otherwise stops as inconclusive.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental-setup}

\subsection{Data and Evaluation}

After a 2,048-token filter, TISER training uses 54,488 items; evaluation uses 19,214 in-domain and 2,800 OOD items. From 15,898 real-entity candidates, the conflict pipeline yields 1,056 conflicts and 120 controls.

\subsection{Tennis Data, Trace, and Parsing Details}

A seed-42, category- and duplicate-group-aware split assigns 785/224/113 of 1,122 records to training/selection/final holdout. Of 785 training candidates, 50 pilot traces are excluded from reported training, 600 disjoint traces train both adapters, and 135 lack generated outputs. File repair wrapped 200 untagged traces and recovered 26 records from 19 malformed lines; semantic validity is audited separately. Evaluation extracts \texttt{<answer>} first, then applies documented fallbacks and tennis-specific normalization. Neither evaluation split has training traces or affects gradients; 224 items select among models, while 113 remain the final holdout, although complete historical non-use cannot be externally verified.

\subsection{Tennis Semantic and Trace Audits}

Two blinded, file-batch model audits cover 1,121 unique semantic payloads (all 1,122 rows) and all 650 traces. Each semantic and trace audit consists of two blinded GPT-5.6 Sol/high-reasoning Codex passes~\cite{openai2026codexapp}, with disagreements and proposed gold changes adjudicated separately. The saved outputs contain labels, evidence quotations, and rationales; automated validation checks identity, schema, and exact quotation, not the correctness of the rationales. The audits do not alter training data, use one uncalibrated model family, and lack verifiable backend/decoding metadata.

\subsection{Models, Training, and Decoding}

Models generate all tags autoregressively; prompt tokens are masked from loss. LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} targets attention $q/k/v/o$. Reproduction is unquantized; continued 7B adaptation uses 4-bit weights. Greedy decoding permits 768 new tokens, except 256 for 7B tennis evaluation.

\subsection{Conflict Sampling and Reflection Audit}

Five recall samples use temperature 0.7/top-$p$ 0.9; three-vote agreement with gold yields 571 eligible sources. Edits produce 203 C1, 520 C2, 333 C3, and 120 controls. The matrix uses one greedy decode per row. Seed-42 row bootstraps (2,000 resamples) and paired McNemar tests do not cluster variants from the same source, so uncertainty may be anti-conservative. A blinded two-pass GPT-5.6 Sol audit labels 2,295 unique nonempty reflections as explicit conflict, no conflict, or unscorable; exact evidence is required for positives and all 66 disagreements are adjudicated. An earlier unverifiable Claude aggregate is excluded.

\subsection{Compute and Software}

Training used one H200 (bf16, about 6.5 h); vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm} evaluation used one H100 (22,014 items in about 17 min). Versions are PyTorch 2.4.1/CUDA 12.4, Transformers 4.46.3, PEFT 0.13.2, TRL 0.12.2, and vLLM 0.22.0/PyTorch 2.11/CUDA 13.0.

\subsection{Extension 3: Retention and Held-Out Generalization}

Retention changes by $-0.004$ macro-EM; its paired interval $[-0.028,0.020]$ establishes neither clear forgetting nor non-inferiority, so replay is not run. Separate OOD EM falls 0.410 to 0.320. On 113 supported holdout items, adaptation raises EM 0.575 to 0.735 ($\Delta=0.159$, 95\% CI 0.080--0.239; McNemar $p=0.000277$) and F1 0.677 to 0.834. Exploratory gains are largest for duration; the small category samples are not confirmatory.

\subsection{Tennis Audit Results}

All 337 evaluation items are model-judge supported. Of 1,121 unique semantic payloads, 1,103 are supported; all 18 concerns occur in training (13 underdetermined, four wrong-gold, one inconsistent), with nine A/B disagreements. Of 650 traces, 632 are supported, 14 have unsupported reasoning, and four contradict the answer, with 12 disagreements. In the reported 600-record training set, 579 pass both audits and 21 have a concern; files remain unchanged. Without retraining, causal effects are unknown, and model judgments are not human certification.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

The reproduction recovers the aggregate in-domain regime, not the original error profile or all baselines and ablations.

Conflict results are descriptive, not causal. TISER-based selection weakens vanilla-model comparisons; C2 can be conspicuous; and row-level statistics ignore variants from shared sources, potentially understating uncertainty. EM is format-sensitive, greedy decoding provides one response per condition, and single-family model audits lack human calibration and reproducible backend settings.

Tennis evidence is limited to synthetic data, one model family, one run per adapter, and 600 traces with 21 audited concerns. The missing unadapted 7B baseline prevents attribution to prior TISER training. The 224-item selection set and 113-item holdout limit precision; historical holdout independence cannot be externally verified. Retention establishes neither forgetting nor non-inferiority, leaving replay untested.

\section{Conclusion}

We reproduce TISER's aggregate regime but retain an unexplained TGQA gap. On a TISER-selected benchmark, structured prompting and TISER weights are associated with greater contextual adherence, while explicit conflict acknowledgement remains rare and order reversal weak. Continued tennis adaptation improves holdout EM from 0.575 to 0.735 over the original adapter. On the 113-item final split, the adapted model retains its selection-set advantage, while original-domain retention remains inconclusive; consequently, the predefined replay branch is not activated. Context use, conflict acknowledgement, domain adaptation, and post-adaptation retention should therefore be evaluated separately.

\section{Reproducibility}

The repository provides command-line data, training, evaluation, audit, and statistical workflows with seed~42, resolved configurations, predictions, metrics, prompts, hashes, provenance, and environment metadata. It retains row-level primary and adjudicated judgments plus a resumable Colab retention/holdout workflow; hashes link each run to its data, adapter, configuration, and scoring view.

\subsection{Adjudication}

Judge A and Judge B were compared by item identity. Follow-up bundles contained
the original source and both decisions for every disagreement, plus every proposed
\emph{wrong gold} decision in the semantic audit. A third independent task,
configured in the UI as \texttt{gpt-5.6-sol} with high reasoning effort,
reapplied the relevant rubric without majority voting. The workflow adjudicated
66 reflection, 12 semantic, and 12 trace decisions. All 90 passed the label,
identity, hash, and exact-quotation checks.
